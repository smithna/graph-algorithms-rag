§1.2 · 1:00

1:00. One takeaway from these three minutes: a JOIN derives the relationship every time you ask, by matching keys. A graph wrote it down. Traversing a million-node graph two hops out touches a few hundred edges and never looks at the rest.

That's the property every algorithm here leans on. PageRank, community detection, path finding — all "follow edges, many times, fast," and only practical because following an edge is a pointer lookup, not a join.

One honest footnote, because the mental picture misleads people later: for several of these, nobody is actually walking. A graph is an adjacency matrix, and "spread mass to my neighbours, repeatedly" is a matrix-vector multiply iterated to convergence — decades of linear algebra optimisation apply. We are not simulating random walks when we compute PageRank; the walk is the proof, not the implementation. Say it once, here, and §5's mass-distribution picture stays a picture instead of becoming a performance claim.

4.8 What WCC is

Say what it is plainly, because "component" sounds like jargon and isn't: start anywhere, follow every edge you can, ignore which way the arrows point, stop when you can't reach anything new. That set is a component. One pass, linear in the edges, no parameters — nothing to tune, nothing to seed.

Run it on the IS_SIMILAR pairs and it turns the bag of pairs into "these ten names are one person" — transitive closure, computed for free, shown to you before any node is merged.

The Sacagawea cluster is built from ten pairwise judgements. No single one of them sees the whole identity.

§6 comes back to this contrast with a receipt: on the retrieval graph WCC returns ONE component containing all 8,139 nodes; Leiden returns 47 communities inside it. Same family, different question.

4.8 The correction

It gets taught as clustering and that is misleading. Run it on a raw corpus graph and you get one giant component and some dust. Here it does the one thing it is exactly right for: transitive closure. A≈B, B≈C, therefore one entity.

§5.3 · damping

At every step, a fraction of the mass teleports back to the seed instead of spreading onward. That fraction is what stops the mass diffusing into the whole graph until it's just a popularity contest — it is exactly what makes this personalized. Set it to 1.0 and there's no teleport: you get plain PageRank, and your seed stops mattering.

So read d as "how far from the seed am I willing to look." Three steps is what you want on a bipartite graph like this one — step 1 is her passages, step 3 is her co-mentioned entities' passages. That's the neighbourhood.

0.85 is the number in every tutorial, including tutorials I have written, and it puts less than half the mass inside that horizon. Measured on this corpus, shorter was better at every value we tried.

The mechanism is the bridge fraction — how much of the seed's edge mass points toward the answer — but you can't observe that before retrieving. Degree you can.
